Overview & Causal Agent
    •    Pathogen: Primarily caused by the fungus Curvularia lunata (and related variants such as C. lunata var. aeria or C. pallescens).
    •    Geographic Distribution & Emergence:
    ◦    United States: Officially reported for the first time in Louisiana in 2017, confirmed in Kentucky in 2018, and subsequently observed across the Corn Belt and Mid-South (including Mississippi and central Iowa in August 2025).
    ◦    Asia (China & India): A major, well-established foliar pathogen. In China, it ranks as the third most destructive corn leaf disease (after Northern and Southern Corn Leaf Blight), causing major epidemics across provinces like Liaoning, Henan, Shandong, and Hebei.
Symptoms & Look-Alikes
    •    Canopy Position: Most commonly appears in the mid- to upper canopy, though it can infect corn at any developmental stage—especially following rain during reproductive maturity.
    •    Lesion Progression:
    ◦    Early Stage: Small, circular-to-oval chlorotic spots with a whitish or light-tan center and a dark-green, water-soaked margin.
    ◦    Mature Stage: Lesions remain relatively small (roughly 1–4 mm long, or up to 1/8" diameter by 1/4" long) with a tan/white necrotic center, a distinct orange-brown to dark-brown border, and an outer translucent yellow (chlorotic) halo.
    ◦    Coalescence: On susceptible hybrids, dense clusters of spots merge into larger irregular necrotic areas.
    •    Key Diagnostic Look-Alike (Eyespot): CLS closely mimics Eyespot (Aureobasidium zeae), which also produces small round lesions with brown rings and yellow halos. However, Eyespot favors cool, wet weather (60s to low 70s °F) and typically appears early in the season prior to pollination, whereas CLS is driven by hot, humid conditions and peaks later in summer.
Epidemiology & Pathogen Biology
    •    Optimal Climate: Thrives in hot and humid weather. Lab characterization in India (Garhwal et al., 2022) identified optimal fungal growth and sporulation at 25°C ± 2°C (77°F) and 80%–90% relative humidity.
    •    Overwintering & Transmission:
    ◦    Crop Residue & Soil: Overwinters as mycelia or conidia in infected corn debris and soil; continuous corn ("corn-on-corn") and high planting densities significantly increase inoculum pressure.
    ◦    Seed-Borne Potential: Studies in India demonstrated high seed-borne transmission rates (40% to 66.7% in tested varieties), alongside a wide alternate host range across weeds and other crops.
    ◦    Spread: Airborne conidia disperse via wind and rain splash, with a latent period of 7–10 days per infection cycle and wind-spread gradients reaching up to 250 meters within a month.
    •    Virulence & Host Genetics (Wang et al., 2022): C. lunata exhibits distinct virulence differentiation across strains. Host resistance in maize is quantitative, governed by 5 to 6 quantitative trait loci (QTLs).
Agronomic Impact & Management
Aspect
United States (Emerging)
China & India (Endemic)
Yield Impact
Historically viewed as largely cosmetic/aesthetic. First U.S. yield losses were documented in 2023, though overall losses remain low and are actively under study.
Severe economic threat. Average yield loss in China is 15.4% (ranging from 1.7% to 36.9%, up to 60% in localized outbreaks); Indian field trials recorded 22.29% yield loss.
Genetic Resistance
Notable differences in hybrid susceptibility have been observed in U.S. field trials, though formal seed-catalog ratings are still developing.
Breeding and deploying QTL-backed resistant varieties is the cornerstone of control.
Cultural Control
Crop rotation away from corn and tillage to bury infected surface residue reduce overwintering inoculum.
Avoiding continuous corn, managing planting density, balancing soil nutrients (avoiding excess nitrogen), and using disease-free seed.
Chemical / Bio Control
No foliar fungicides are currently labeled specifically for Curvularia leaf spot in the U.S.
Managed using targeted fungicides and biological control agents guided by pollination and grain-fill disease-index forecasting models.
